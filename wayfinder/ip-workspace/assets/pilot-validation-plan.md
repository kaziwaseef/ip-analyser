# Pilot-validation plan

Draft for the final decision, [What representative product and acceptance criteria will validate this workflow?](../issues/09-pilot-validation.md), 2026-10-19. A real product has not yet been chosen. No searches or product assessments have been performed by this plan.

## Pilot choice to settle with the user

- Real physical product: not supplied. A name and available description, supplier listing, photographs, or documents can start intake.
- Proposed first market: US, following the accepted default.
- Proposed objective: validate the end-to-end scoped IP research/risk workflow in stages, beginning with intake and the detailed external patent-search-AI prompt. Continue with external result import, verification, comparisons, challenges, and reporting when the required material is available.
- Actual product-specific rights/workstreams, variants, dates, evidence, exclusions, search depth, and stopping rule: establish during scoped intake. Do not infer every workstream applies or declare coverage complete by elapsed time or result count.

## Proposed acceptance criteria

1. Create the correct product workspace and overview, ask focused questions, and preserve unknowns rather than filling them with assumptions.
2. Produce a self-contained external patent-search prompt and attachment checklist tied to the product revision and US scope, with a saved handoff manifest.
3. Preserve a returned external report and normalize candidates and query provenance. Verify material originals and status, retaining unsupported execution claims, inaccessible records, missing views, and uncertain translations as explicit gaps.
4. Produce traceable comparisons for the actual scoped workstreams, including a limitation-level patent comparison and original-view design comparison when relevant. Retain unregistered-right and provenance gaps where applicable.
5. Demonstrate different challenge approaches and a sensitivity case. Show adverse evidence honestly, with 1–5/U concern, separate exposure/confidence/coverage, and residual risk even in favorable results.
6. Show that a material product change starts a new relevant assessment while preserving the earlier run. Check separate-market isolation without assuming a second market's substantive assessment is authorized.
7. Keep overview, next actions, decision records, and changelog consistent. Check that an eleventh change folds the oldest recent entry into the cumulative final old-change summary without losing prior summarized substance.
8. Produce an internal scoped report with limitations and explicit escalations. Keep any actual legal review and user business decision separate. Do not infer approval from a score.

## Difficult cases without fabricated findings

The workflow must handle a difficult claim, incomplete design images, a possible unregistered right, unknown legal status, a missing product feature, adverse evidence, and a revision that changes earlier applicability. Use actual product evidence when such cases arise. If a real case is absent, test the process through clearly labeled, isolated hypothetical validation scenarios. Never insert synthetic rights, claims, facts, risk scores, or approvals into the real product's evidence or findings.

Separate-market and changelog rollover checks can use throwaway fixtures, which must not become a claimed real market analysis or actual product history. Preserve only the validation result and its stated limits in the validation record.

## Completion and limits

Record each criterion as passed, failed, incomplete, or not applicable with a reason and evidence. Fix workflow defects exposed by the pilot before calling it validated. External results or missing technical evidence can leave stages pending while the intake/handoff stage remains useful.

The pilot-selection decision resolves when the user chooses the product/scope and acceptance approach. Actual pilot execution and any assessment completion remain separate work with their real prerequisites; this planning artifact is not evidence of success.
