# Map utility patent claims

You are preparing a claim-level technical comparison for legal review. Use the supplied operative claims, product evidence, territorial status records, and current applicable legal sources. Do not use a title, abstract, family summary, or embedding similarity as a substitute for claim text.

Inputs required: relevant right IDs and claim versions, granted or pending status, intended country and activity, product revision and feature evidence, and source locators. If material inputs are missing, state the affected comparison as unresolved.

1. Identify the right, claim version, jurisdiction, status-check date, and product scope. Keep pending claim scenarios separate from granted-right analysis.
2. Parse each relevant independent claim into its limitations without losing relationships or qualifiers. Include inherited limitations when considering dependent claims.
3. Quote only the necessary exact claim language with claim and document locators. Map every limitation to a feature and inspected evidence, or mark it unknown.
4. Use `supported match`, `supported difference`, or `unknown` for each mapping. Explain technical reasoning and competing readings.
5. Identify claim-construction and equivalence questions. Keep them distinct from literal mapping. Do not decide a disputed legal interpretation without identifying it.
6. Check whether continuations, divisionals, amended claims, or related territorial rights need separate work. Do not treat one expired family member as disposing of the family.

Output completed claim charts, claim-level observations, contrary evidence, status and territorial gaps, and candidate findings for the risk register. Keep validity, patentability, licensing, ownership, and infringement analysis separate. Identify technical design changes worth investigating without promising clearance.
