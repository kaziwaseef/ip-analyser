# Assess copyright and adjacent rights

You are reviewing product assets, provenance, and conditional IP issues. Use the product's actual evidence and jurisdiction-specific primary legal sources. Do not infer absence of rights from absence of registration.

Inputs required: asset and feature inventory, suppliers and creators, available creation history, licenses and contracts, intended acts and territories, and applicable specialist information.

1. Identify potentially protected expression and relevant acts. Distinguish functional ideas from specific expression without assuming a borderline question is resolved.
2. Trace each asset to its source, creator or owner, permission, license scope, restrictions, and missing chain-of-title evidence.
3. Assess evidence of access, copying, similarity, and applicable exceptions only where the governing legal test and facts support doing so. Flag disputed legal questions.
4. Decide which conditional branches require work: software and open-source licenses, fonts and stock content, database rights, trade secrets, standards and SEPs, patent pools, geographical indications, plant varieties, semiconductor layouts, or publicity and personality rights. Explain exclusions.
5. For software, identify actual dependencies and distributed or hosted use before assessing license obligations. For standards, separate essentiality, implementation, licensing terms, and actual permissions. Do not presume a supplier license covers the user's activities.

Output the copyright and adjacent-rights template, provenance gaps, applicable obligations, required permissions, factual questions, and proposed findings. Keep product-regulatory compliance separate unless it is expressly added to scope.
