# Find and preserve rights evidence

Read the product's intake, feature list, applicable legal foundations, and research-system reference. Keep discovery and verification as separate steps.

Confirm that the user's objective and work scope are explicit. If they are not, ask before searching. Limit workstreams to that scope. For patent-related work, prepare the product-specific external-AI prompt through [the handoff workflow](external-patent-search.md) after intake. Import results from that AI or other tools with their provenance, then independently verify material documents and status before analysis.

1. Build a search plan by right type and territory in `search-log.md`. Start with product language, technical synonyms, components, competitors, known owners, and relevant classifications.
2. Search patents by technical features, classification, assignee, inventors, citations, and related applications. Search designs using images, product categories, owners, and classifications. Search marks for similar signs, goods and services, and actual marketplace use.
3. Record every material query with the database, exact syntax, filters, date, searcher, result count when available, and saved-result location. Record unavailable sources and no-result searches too.
4. Create candidate entries in `rights.md`. Preserve publication and application identifiers, kind codes, family relationships, jurisdiction, and apparent relevance. Deduplicate references without collapsing territorial rights or claim versions.
5. Obtain full material documents. Save originals under `evidence/raw/` and register them in `evidence/index.md`. Record image views and document locators, not just a landing-page URL.
6. Verify the relevant granted or pending claim version, prosecution changes, ownership, status, territorial effect, and dates using appropriate official sources. Record each check's date and any conflicting evidence.
7. Follow family members, continuations, divisionals, citations, national validations, and related design or trademark records where the evidence warrants it.
8. Search beyond registrations for applicable unregistered designs, trademark use, trade dress, copyright, and provenance. Record what could not be assessed by a register search.
9. Record exclusions and why they do not need further work within the defined scope. Preserve their IDs. Mark uncertain exclusions for review.
10. Identify remaining searches, source limits, and refresh needs. Hand the evidence inventory to analysis with explicit gaps.

As material evidence, candidate rights, exclusions, or blockers change, update the product's `overview.md` and changelog in the same work step. Follow [the common changelog rules](maintain-changelogs.md). Keep detailed queries and source records in their respective registers.

Treat a publication as a document and a right as a territorial legal interest. Several documents may concern one application. One family can contain several rights with different claims and status.

Do not rely only on an aggregator's legal-status flag. If the authoritative record requires an account or is unavailable, record that blocker. Never replace an unavailable status check with a favorable assumption.

## Search coverage

Describe coverage qualitatively by workstream and territory. Record the search concepts, classification expansion, owner and family follow-up, evidence inspected, exclusions, and unresolved access limits. A large number of hits is not proof of coverage.

The search stopping rule must fit the product and review scope. Until it is agreed, label the search exploratory. Include the possibility of unpublished applications, unregistered rights, indexing delays, translation limits, and incomplete product facts where relevant.
